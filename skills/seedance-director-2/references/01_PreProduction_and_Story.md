# 01 — Pre-Production & Story

## Four pre-production questions

Ask only what is missing and materially affects the prompt. Reuse answers and approvals already present.

1. **Length:** ask the intended duration unless deliberately skipped or direct delivery was requested. Match action density to time. Check exact model/platform support before recommending a technical mode; do not infer numeric limits from this guide.
2. **Reference, Axis A:** use supplied opening images, first/last frames, identity images, motion videos or audio. Generate a reference only when requested, per doc 02. Map multiple references per doc 04; simple opening-image work remains untagged.
3. **Editing, Axis B:** continuous single shot or multiple shots? Neither is a default. Honor the user’s stated choice. Otherwise make a scene-based directing decision and briefly explain it; ask one concise question if the tradeoff is materially ambiguous. Reference count does not decide editing.
4. **Intent:** understand what the viewer should perceive or feel. Quiet waiting, listening, a restrained reaction and pure movement are valid goals.

Ask about exact timing only when a handoff, entrance, impact, cut or dialogue requires it. Direct audio when useful or requested; do not add compulsory ambience, music or silence.

## Story development

If inventing a new narrative scene and the user has not approved its direction, propose a short paragraph in the user's language for feedback. Do not insert an approval gate when a clear scene, script or direction is already supplied, or when the user asks for direct delivery.

Use this five-part arc as an optional development aid for a scene that needs one:
- Starting point: where we are.
- Desire: what the character wants.
- Conflict: tension or obstacle.
- Change: a turning point.
- Result: a meaningful consequence.

Do not force the entire arc into every shot. For extended sequences, divide events into readable stages only as useful. For pure action, proceed with the stated character, action and energy. For acting, connect thought, feeling, intention and visible behavior without inventing unrequested story content.

## Opening-image check

Check pose, available space, visible props, composition and the moment at which the action begins. If the image shows the action already completed or cannot support its spatial logic, propose fixing the image before repeatedly expanding the prompt. A reference supplies identity, lighting, scene and style; prompt the necessary changes rather than redescribing it.

## Choose editing structure deliberately

- Evaluate the script, duration, emotional turns, listener reactions, reveals, spatial continuity and comic timing before writing the prompt. A longer scene needs explicit consideration of coverage and pacing; duration alone mandates neither mode.
- Choose multi-shot when changes in viewpoint, shot size, listener reactions or reveals advance the scene. Cut on a change in thought, emotion or information, rather than automatically on each dialogue line.
- Choose a continuous shot when uninterrupted time, shared space, sustained tension or readable choreography provides a concrete dramatic benefit. Do not select it just because cuts were not requested.
- If both treatments are equally plausible and the preference would materially change the result, ask: “Would you like one continuous shot or an edited multi-shot scene?” If the user requests direct delivery or delegates direction, decide from the available scene and state the choice briefly.
- Requests for minimal actor actions, concise prompts or thought-and-feeling-based acting do not imply a continuous shot, locked camera or reduced editorial coverage.
