# Prop continuity and physical obstacles

Apply this module when the scene depends on carrying, handing over, throwing, hiding, replacing or releasing a prop, or crossing a physical opening. Skip it for scenes without these risks. Perform the checks internally; do not add a compulsory checklist or LOCK template to the delivered prompt.

## Track only consequential states

- Distinguish similar props when they could merge or substitute for one another. Track each important object's identity, location, holder and state before and after critical actions, including when offscreen.
- Define where an object remains after release, concealment or abandonment when later continuity depends on it.
- Make prerequisite changes explicit: release the branch, then cross the opening with an empty mouth. Never allow the later action to imply an unmentioned transfer or disappearance.
- Repeat only the fragile state at its critical transition. Avoid restating every constraint in every beat.

## Stage the physical mechanism

- Define the relative dimensions, orientation, rigidity and actual contact points needed for the obstacle. Exact measurements are optional; meaningful spatial relationships are essential.
- Couple contact to a visible whole-body consequence and a concrete location. For example: “The rigid branch hits both gateposts, abruptly stopping the dog's entire body outside the gateway. Its front paws plant on the outer grass. Still gripping the branch, it takes two steps back toward the bushes.”
- Describe contact geometry accurately. A branch wider than an opening may contact the posts along its shaft while its tips extend beyond them; do not automatically say its tips hit the posts.
- Choose framing that makes the obstruction, contact and stopped body readable. Check opening-image feasibility and choose continuous-shot or cut structure deliberately.

## Diagnose violated constraints

Treat this as a writing and staging heuristic, not proof of reliable model physics. A user-reported dog sequence explicitly required a rigid branch, blocked passage and a stopped dog, yet the branch contacted the posts and then passed through them. Contact rendered correctly did not mean collision response was respected. STRICT/LOCK headings are organizational labels, not enforcement mechanisms.

When the model violates an already explicit constraint, do not assume missing wording or keep stacking prohibitions. First identify the failed transition: contact, whole-body stop, grip, release or later passage. Test a focused causal rewrite with visible body response and spatial anchoring. If failure persists, simplify or isolate the collision in a shorter shot, change staging or provide a suitable reference. Change one relevant factor at a time when diagnosing. Present proposed fixes as hypotheses until results support them; never claim this prompt structure has been validated merely because it is detailed.
