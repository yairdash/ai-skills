# Finishing, QC, and troubleshooting

## Diagnose before regenerating

Name the primary failure, then change one main variable on the next attempt.

| Failure class | Typical symptom | First response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | face or age shifts | use the clearest identity reference; remove conflicting references |
| Wardrobe/prop | design, logo, material, or geometry changes | use a dedicated item reference and name the immutable traits |
| Composition | subject misplaced or opening image differs | prepare/repair a start image; use image-to-video for exact frame one |
| Action | motion is incomplete or incoherent | reduce to one chronological beat and make it the first sentence |
| Camera | unwanted pan/cuts/drift | specify one stable framing and one move; remove competing camera language |
| Audio | line is unclear or sync feels weak | shorten the line; simplify performance; plan an external dialogue replacement if critical |
| Join | extension resets action or visibly degrades | restart from a strong keyframe or cut to a new shot instead of extending further |

## Select takes deliberately

Choose a take against the shot's purpose, not merely the most spectacular frame. Record the selected version, why it was selected, and its usable in/out range. Keep alternative takes only if they solve a distinct editorial problem.

## Handoff to an editor

For each selected shot, provide:

```text
SHOT ID:
Selected file and version:
Usable in / out:
Purpose in the cut:
Reference or continuity notes:
Sound status: keep native / replace dialogue / replace all sound
Required post: grade, title, subtitle, VFX fix, or none
```

Export individual selected clips when final pacing, music, dialogue, titles, transitions, or color work matter. Do not bake a hard-to-change stitched sequence merely for convenience.

## Final continuity review

Review shots in edit order, not one by one:

- Does the character's identity, hair, wardrobe, and scale hold?
- Are hero props and graphic details consistent when visible?
- Do location geography, lighting direction, and time of day remain understandable?
- Are screen direction, eyelines, and action progression coherent across the cut?
- Is the dialogue intelligible and assigned to the correct speaker?
- Do joins sound and look intentional?

Fix the most narratively visible fault first. A clean insert, reaction, or cutaway often protects a scene better than re-generating an entire otherwise-successful shot.
