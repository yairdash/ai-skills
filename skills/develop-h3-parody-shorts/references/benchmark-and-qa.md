# Benchmark and QA

The dollar figures below are an illustrative budget using a dated $0.26/second assumption, not current pricing or spending authorization. Refresh the live rate and recompute before any paid job.

## Lean benchmark

- Pass A: six 5-second 16:9 minimal-anchor T2V probes; expansion off; $7.80.
- Pass B: six structured triple-anchor probes; expansion off; $7.80.
- Pass C: up to three targeted expansion or reference rescues; at most $3.90.
- Pass D: up to three controls for variance, 9:16 distortion, and reference-vs-memory; at most $3.90.

Maximum planned spend: $23.40. Stop when an earlier pass resolves the hypothesis.

Score 0–4 for visual identity, character behavior, voice/cadence, world/set recognition, dialogue/lip-sync, temporal consistency, and unwanted blending. For unwanted blending, 4 is clean separation. Save evidence for every score and inspect full playback plus beginning, middle, and ending frames.

Classify as `native-reliable` when identity, behavior, and world/set are each at least 3 without references; `native-partial` when one critical dimension needs structure; `reference-required` when references are necessary; or `unreliable` after failed rescue. Do not average away a critical failure.

A rerun must answer a named uncertainty. Change one variable where possible. Poll or recover an existing request ID; never create a second billable retry for it.

## Production-transfer check

Do not promote a character solely because a five-second probe is recognizable. Before production use, test or reason separately about sustained identity, behavior and cadence, set recall, speaker/listener separation, prop continuity, reaction timing, and aspect-ratio stability.

Record user qualitative review as `project-empirical` evidence, but do not invent numerical scores after the fact. A weak production short can reflect property recall, scene complexity, reference conflicts, prompt execution, or random variance; preserve that uncertainty.
